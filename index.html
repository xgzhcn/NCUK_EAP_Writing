Topic A

"AI (artificial intelligence) technology is slowly taking over more and more jobs that could be done by humans. Governments should make laws now to protect jobs from this new technology. To what extent do you agree?"

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has sparked widespread concern regarding its impact on the global workforce. As AI systems become increasingly capable of performing complex tasks previously reserved for humans, some argue that governments must enact strict legislation to protect existing jobs. While I agree that governments have a crucial role to play in managing this economic transition, I strongly disagree with the notion of implementing protectionist laws aimed at halting technological progress.

On the one hand, the threat AI poses to employment is undeniable. Automation is no longer restricted to manual labour; it now encompasses cognitive tasks in sectors such as finance, healthcare, and customer service. Without intervention, this disruption could lead to significant unemployment and widen economic inequality. Therefore, government action is necessary. However, this action should take the form of robust social safety nets rather than bans on technology. By investing heavily in education, vocational retraining, and exploring concepts like universal basic income, governments can equip displaced workers with the skills needed to thrive in an evolving, tech-centric economy.

Conversely, enacting laws to explicitly protect jobs from AI would be counterproductive. Historically, resisting technological innovation stifles economic growth and reduces a nation's global competitiveness. If a government bans the use of AI in certain industries to save jobs, companies within that nation will inevitably fall behind international competitors who leverage AI for greater efficiency and innovation. Furthermore, while AI displaces certain roles, it simultaneously creates new industries and occupations that currently do not exist.

In conclusion, while the displacement of jobs by AI is a valid concern requiring immediate attention, protective legislation is the wrong approach. Governments should instead focus on proactive adaptation, prioritizing the education and financial security of their citizens to ensure a smooth transition into an AI-integrated future.

Topic B

"English should be the official world language. No other languages are necessary. To what extent do you agree with this statement?"

In our highly globalized world, English has undeniably established itself as the dominant lingua franca, facilitating international trade, diplomacy, and scientific research. Because of its utility, some propose that English should be adopted as the sole official world language, rendering all other languages obsolete. I completely disagree with this assertion, as eradicating linguistic diversity would result in a catastrophic loss of cultural heritage and cognitive diversity.

Admittedly, the widespread use of English offers significant practical advantages. A shared global language breaks down communication barriers, streamlining international business operations and allowing for the seamless exchange of academic and scientific knowledge. It enables people from vastly different geographical backgrounds to collaborate efficiently. However, acknowledging English as a useful common tool is entirely different from suggesting it should be the only language in existence.

The primary flaw in the argument for a single world language is its disregard for the profound connection between language and culture. A language is not merely a mechanical tool for transferring information; it is a repository of a community's history, values, idioms, and unique worldview. When a language dies, the cultural identity and ancestral knowledge embedded within it are lost forever. Furthermore, linguistic diversity benefits human cognition. Research has consistently shown that bilingualism and multilingualism enhance brain plasticity, improve problem-solving skills, and foster a greater capacity for empathy by allowing individuals to view the world through multiple linguistic frameworks.

In conclusion, while English serves an incredibly vital role as a global medium of communication, the suggestion that no other languages are necessary is fundamentally flawed. We should actively celebrate and preserve our linguistic diversity, promoting a world where individuals are multilingual, preserving their cultural roots while simultaneously participating in the global community.
